# Code of conduct

This project is small. The rules are correspondingly short, and they are the
project's own words rather than a copy of a standard text.

## What is expected

Be straightforward and be kind. Assume the person you are talking to is acting
in good faith and is probably busy.

Technical disagreement is welcome and useful. Argue about the code, the design,
or the evidence. Do not argue about the person.

Say what you actually observed. "This fails for me on Node 20, here is the
output" is worth more than "this is broken". A bug report that says what you
expected, what happened, and how to reproduce it will get attention; one that
says the project is bad will not.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, including in issues, pull requests,
  commit messages, and code.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments about anyone's identity or background.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments, demanding free labour, or
  pressuring maintainers with deadlines they never agreed to.

## Scope

This applies anywhere the project is being discussed: issues, pull requests,
discussions, and commits.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may edit, hide, or delete comments, close issues and pull
requests, and block accounts. For anything serious, or to report behaviour
privately, use GitHub's report-abuse flow rather than a public issue.

Enforcement here is one person's judgment applied in good faith. If you think a
decision was wrong, say so once, plainly, and accept the outcome.
